Design

This is the new analysis. The old one (EU/RoW and North/South under four closures, 96 runs) is under the Old toggle, unchanged.

What is fixed

  • One world: North/South. The North carries out each of the twelve interventions (sc1 to sc12) from period 70.
  • One settlement: fiat. Exchange rate fixed; the reserve-holding central bank's stock of the other region's bills absorbs the balance of payments.
  • Responses are the mean over periods 79 to 100 of each run's difference from its own run without the intervention.

What varies (1): who adjusts

No rule. Nobody adjusts. Each government follows its normal spending path. A reserve gap opened by the intervention is never closed: fiat reserves earn interest, so the gap keeps growing slowly.

A fiscal rule. Each period, the adjusting government:

  • looks at its reserves: its central bank's net holdings of the other region's bills;
  • compares them with a reference: where its reserves would have been, in the same period, had there been no intervention (taken from the run without the intervention);
  • if reserves are below the reference (the intervention is draining them), it cuts real spending in proportion to the shortfall;
  • if reserves are above the reference (the intervention is adding to them), it raises real spending in proportion to the excess. This is the symmetric rule. (A deficit-only version, where only the losing side cuts and the gaining side does nothing, was also run but is not shown here.)
  • does nothing when there is no intervention: without one, reserves equal the reference, so every rule run without an intervention is identical to the no-rule run.

Who can adjust:

  • the South alone;
  • the North alone;
  • both, sharing the burden in proportion to the size of each government (North spending is about three times the South's), so both change spending by the same percentage.

How fast:

  • every rule closes a reserve gap at the same speed: half of the gap is gone after about four periods;
  • the North needs a stronger response than the South to reach that speed, because Northern government purchases are less import-intensive (a unit of Northern spending moves the North's current account about two-thirds as much as a unit of Southern spending moves the South's);
  • holding the speed equal means the cases differ only in who adjusts, not in how fast or how much reserves move.

What varies (2): whose money is the reserve

  • North money ("N-res", the actual world): the South's central bank holds North government bills as its reserve.
  • South money ("S-res", a counterfactual): the North's central bank holds South government bills.

ANOVA

How much of the variation in the 74 responses comes from the intervention, from who adjusts, and from their combination. Each response is put on a common scale and the six outcome blocks (activity, fiscal, external, labour and distribution, finance and wealth, ecological) weigh equally. One run per combination, so these are exact shares of this grid, not statistical tests. Each bar is split by the intervention that carries it.

How to read this without drawing false conclusions

  • "On its own" means "in the same direction for every intervention". The share of a factor on its own measures how far it moves all twelve interventions the same way. A small share does not mean the factor does not matter.
  • Effects with opposite signs cancel in that average. Who adjusts pushes results one way when the intervention worsens the North's current account (production leakage) and the opposite way when it improves it (competitive displacement). Averaged over interventions, the two cancel, and the effect is moved into the interaction term.
  • Test: relabel the rules by role. Calling the rules "deficit region adjusts" and "surplus region adjusts" instead of "North" and "South" makes the effect point the same way in every intervention. The share of who adjusts on its own then rises from 0.3% to 4.5% (second figure).
  • What remains is a difference in size, not in sign. Even with role labels, the interaction stays at 27.8% (against 32.1% with North/South labels), because the effect is large where the intervention opens a large imbalance (sc1, sc8, sc12) and tiny where it opens almost none.
  • So: read the interaction, and look at who carries it (the colours). The interaction here is not noise or a curiosity: it is the main way who adjusts acts.

Groups

Each group is named after the response that most distinguishes it and its dominant transmission regime. The chips are the five most distinctive responses: ▲ rise, ▼ fall (green / red when the group moves further than average, with a strength word by distance from the average: slight under 0.5 sd, mild 0.5-1, pronounced 1-1.5, sharp 1.5-2.5, very sharp 2.5 and above); grey hollow arrows when the group moves the same way as the average but less ("milder", or "much milder" beyond 1 sd). Values are the group's mean response and the mean of all runs in the set; "sd" is how far the group sits from that mean, in standard deviations. Responses are in % of the run without the intervention, points (pp) for shares and ratios, or % of value added for balances.

PCA

Two steps. First the interventions alone, with nobody adjusting. Then what each fiscal rule does to them.

1. No rule: the twelve interventions under each hierarchy

PC1 (59% of the variation) is the external and fiscal imbalance the intervention opens: South current account balance (-0.28); North current account balance (+0.28); North trade balance (+0.28); South trade balance (-0.28); North government deficit (-0.22); South government deficit (+0.22); South debt to GDP ratio (+0.22); South government debt (+0.22). PC2 (24%) is the distribution and finance side: South household demand for personal loans (+0.30); North household demand for personal loans (+0.24); North net household wealth (+0.23); South worker/capitalist income gap |1 - ydw/ydc| (rises when workers gain) (-0.23); South wage share of disposable income (-0.23); South profit share of disposable income (+0.23); North worker/capitalist income gap |1 - ydw/ydc| (rises when workers gain) (-0.21); North wage share of disposable income (-0.21).

Filled circles are the actual world (North money is the reserve); triangles are the same intervention if South money were the reserve, joined by a dotted line. Production leakage (sc1, sc12) sits on one side of PC1, competitive displacement (sc8, sc2) on the other, contained contraction near the centre. The counterfactual hierarchy moves the large-imbalance interventions a long way (sc1 HH-Food becomes dual expansion, sc8 Int-Metal dual contraction) and the others hardly at all: with nobody adjusting, a fiat reserve gap compounds, and whose money is the reserve decides on which side it compounds.

2. What each rule does (North money)

Arrows run from the no-rule run to the same intervention under each rule. In this plane North adjustment pulls the imbalance back towards the centre along PC1, and South adjustment moves results mostly along PC2. But only 21% of the movement lies in this plane: the rules move results mostly in a direction the interventions themselves do not vary along. The Who adjusts tab draws that direction.

Who adjusts

For each intervention, take the difference between "North adjusts" and "South adjusts", and re-sign it so it runs from "the surplus region adjusts" to "the deficit region adjusts" (the deficit region is the one whose current account the intervention worsens). The twelve differences lie on one direction: 99.6% of their variation. That direction is world demand: it loads on South government spending; North government spending; North real final imports; South real final exports; South tax payments; North total real consumption; South final demand in real terms; North waste; North gross output; North final demand in real terms.

  • The surplus region adjusts → world demand expands. The deficit region adjusts → it contracts. Both adjusting nets to about zero.
  • The size of the move grows with the imbalance (the distance from x = 0). That is why sc1, sc8 and sc12 dominated every earlier measure.
  • The regime decides which region is in deficit. Under leakage the North is in deficit, so "North adjusts" is contractionary. Under displacement the South is, and the signs flip. That is the cancellation in the average.

Which region is the surplus region?

It depends on the intervention, through its regime. In production leakage (sc1 HH-Food, sc12 Int-Food, sc5 Int-Wood) the South gains reserves, so the South is the surplus region and South adjustment is the expansionary one. In competitive displacement (sc8 Int-Metal, sc2 HH-Energy) the North gains reserves, so North adjustment is the expansionary one. This is Keynes's point in the clearing-union proposal: if only deficit countries adjust, adjustment is deflationary for everyone; if surplus countries adjust, it is expansionary. The model reproduces it, and adds that a circular-economy intervention decides which country is the surplus country.

Exception: sc11 Int-Energy does not follow the common direction. Not yet checked: the ecological side (Southern emissions fall when the deficit region adjusts; loading -0.13).
