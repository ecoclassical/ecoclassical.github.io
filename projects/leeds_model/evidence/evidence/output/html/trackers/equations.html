What this tracks. Every change to the model's equations between the description Marco submitted (submitted_marco, the docx, which cannot be run) and the canonical model the revision reports (arm revised), and then every equation each external closure changes against revised. The matrix rows are the docx's thirteen blocks; its columns are the steps of the path, then the closures, then three side branches that are not on the path.

One item is one (step, block) pair. Its count says how many equations of that block the step touched, split into changed (a different form), new (the step's column has a form, the one before had none) and dropped. The counts are computed from qmd/equations/equations.csv by comparing each arm's column with its parent arm's column, not with the column to its left on the page, because the page's column order is not the lineage: lambda descends from adjudicated, not from inflation.

The path, measured. submitted_marco → submitted → customs → adjudicated → lambda → sraffian → revised_r12 → revised. The catalogue has 152 equation rows. 84 differ between Marco's docx and revised: 36 have a changed form, 45 are equations the docx does not have (the code's reporting equations, the price-of-production system, the Armington and Tobin blocks), and 3 are equations revised drops (12.6, 12.16, 12.17).

Two edits change no algebra and so have no items: waste_treatment (it changes which sectors 11.1 and 11.2 read, not their form) and sfc_flow_of_funds (it changes evaluation order). Both are in data/edits.csv.

Closed — closures implemented against revised

4/4 closed · 100%

Each closure is defined by the equations it changes in revised; every other equation is copied from revised (tools/melt_closure_tables.py), so the closures page cannot drift from the catalogue. All closures run in the main run only; the spin-up always runs OPENFIX-gold. The financial dynamics each closure leaves are recorded in log/trackers/closure_region.md. Here the question is only which equations change, and how each change maps onto Godley and Lavoie (2007, ch. 12).

OPENFIX-fiat (G&L 12.82A, 12.83, 12.84, 12.89F–12.91F). Gold is fixed. The EU central bank's holdings of RoW bills become the residual of the RoW bill market (10.5, 12.90F), and each central bank's own-bill holding follows its balance sheet in flow form (10.2, 10.3). 10.6 is the redundant equation again. The reserves earn RoW interest, which is the compounding root R(t+1) = (1 + r_b) R(t) + f that G&L describe (p. 467). On 2026-10-01 the maintainer chose to calibrate it to a zero current account, not to add a reaction rule; that is a calibration, so it changes no equation here.

OPENFIXG (G&L 12.82AG). All of the adjusting region's reserves are fixed, its bill supply is the sum of the demands at the fixed rates (5.6a), the deficit is what that market absorbs (5.5), and real government spending is the residual of the budget constraint (5.1 inverted). The adjusting region is the one whose central bank holds the other's bills: the EU in EU/RoW, the South in North/South (open question 36).

Quasi-float (Marco's rule, no G&L counterpart). One new equation: 10.0, xr = xr(−1) + par_xr · CAB(−1)/YN(−1). All its terms are lagged, so it adds no simultaneous loop. Gold still settles.

In flight — OPENFLEX: the equations run, the closure is unstable

0/1 closed · 0%

OPENFLEX (G&L 12.83, 12.84FL, 12.89FL–12.91FL). Both central banks' reserves are fixed (10.2, 10.3, 10.7, 10.8). The supply of RoW bills to EU households is the residual of the RoW bill market (10.4, 12.90FL), and the exchange rate is the price that clears it (10.0, 12.89FL). The equations are as G&L write them. Two things that are not in G&L's equations had to be added to make the closure solve.

  • A solver, not an equation. The rate is found by a secant search inside each period, because the EU's holdings of RoW bills (about 66) are thin against the market (about 3,000).
  • An expectation, which is an equation. The portfolio block now reads the currency gain with adaptive expectations at θ = 1: last period's realised gain, in place of the same period's. With the same period's gain, the loop was positive and diverged. This change to the returns of 7.1–7.4 is not yet a row of closure_overrides.csv, so the closures page does not show it (see Loose ends).

At θ = 1 and σ = 1 every period solves, but the rate cycles with growing amplitude and the run stops at t = 24. It is an expectations cobweb, not G&L's decelerating depreciation.
