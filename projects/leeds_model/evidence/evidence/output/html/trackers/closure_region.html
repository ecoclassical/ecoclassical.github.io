What the grid is. Six Godley–Lavoie external closures (Godley & Lavoie 2007, Monetary Economics, ch. 12) crossed with two regional workbooks:

  • EU/RoW: the canonical revised, on the EU-27 at its 2020 membership;
  • North/South: the EU at its 2011 membership inside the North; China and Russia in the South.

That makes 12 cells, of which 2 (OPENFIXR) are excluded. A third workbook, EU2011/RoW (the EU-27 at its 2011 membership, the UK in), was dropped on 2026-10-01; see "Dropped" below. A cell is complete when:

  • 1. its baseline is stationary and calibrated: |debt/VA slope| < 1e-4 per period, |CAB/VA| < 1e-3 at t = 100, and debt/VA and VA at their 2011 targets;
  • 2. the 12 circular-economy scenarios (all but construction, scenario 14) have been run on it.

Complete scenario coverage: canonical EU/RoW OPENFIX-gold and OPENFIX-fiat on both regional setups. Each has twelve accepted included caches in output/calibration/scenario_grid_20261001_status.csv. The other ready cells are being extended. This is observed coverage, not proof of attraction; the fiat cases remain conditional experiments from calibrated knife-edge baselines.

The monetary hierarchy is a separate axis (maintainer, 2026-10-01; open question 36). The workbook varies regional membership only. The hierarchy is the arm column reserve_spec: RoW (the dollar world) issues world money on EU/RoW (z1_holds_z2), and the North does on North/South (z2_holds_z1). It acts only under OPENFIX-fiat (whose bills are the reserve asset) and OPENFIXG (who adjusts). Under OPENFIX-gold and the quasi-float, gold settles and the hierarchy is inert, so those are the neutral cells. The reversed hierarchy (EU money as world money) is an optional extra arm on the canonical workbook, not a regional setup. So the three monetary orders the grid compares are: neutral (OPENFIX-gold on EU/RoW, the canonical model), RoW > EU (fiat and OPENFIXG on EU/RoW) and North > South (the North/South arms).

Closed — stationary, calibrated baselines

8/8 closed · 100%

These eight cells have a baseline that stays put (two of them, under OPENFIX-fiat, only as a knife-edge). Each closure gets there by a different financial route, and the route matters for how a scenario's results are read.

OPENFIX-gold (R6, the canonical closure). Central banks settle the balance of payments in gold.

  • What holds and what absorbs. The exchange rate is fixed, and the central banks' gold absorbs every external imbalance.
  • The unit root. With the current account at zero, the government budget balances too. Public debt and gold then share a unit root: the level at which debt settles depends on the path, not on any flow parameter.
  • How it is calibrated. By an initial condition, the debt endowment: bills issued to the region's own central bank at the start of the spin-up.
  • Reading a scenario. A scenario that moves the current account drains or accumulates gold without limit, and public debt follows. So under gold, the change in public debt is the cumulated external-balance effect. The stationary baseline is what makes that change readable.

OPENFIXG (G&L §12.6.2). The adjusting region keeps its reserves fixed, and its real government spending is the residual of its budget constraint.

  • What it guarantees. The current account goes to zero and both debt ratios stabilise. G&L show this in Fig. 12.3D, and it is the one fixed-rate closure where they do.
  • Its cost, in the book. A "deflationary bias to the world economy as a whole": the deficit country's output falls, and so in the end does the surplus country's.
  • Our rule for which region adjusts (open question 36): the region whose central bank holds the other's bills. That is the EU on the canonical model and the South on North/South.
  • Reading a scenario. The external effect of a scenario shows up as a change in the adjusting region's government spending, not in its debt.

Quasi-float (Marco's crawling rule; par_xr). The North/EU rate moves by par_xr × lagged CAB/YN; settlement stays in gold.

  • Why it is stationary now. Since Armington on intermediates and the two exchange-rate conversion fixes (2026-09-30), the baseline is stationary. Before them, an appreciation raised the EU trade balance, so the rate ran away.
  • What is not yet known. Stationary baseline at par_xr = 0.15, but its stability after a disturbance is untested. Measured growth of |xr − 1| per period over t = 71–100: −0.07% at par_xr 0.05, +0.05% at 0.15, +0.46% at 0.5 (λ plan, Task 5).

OPENFIX-fiat (G&L's OPENFIX proper, §12.5). Gold is fixed, and the reserve-holding central bank's stock of the other region's bills absorbs the balance of payments. The reserves earn interest, so they obey R(t+1) = (1 + r_b) R(t) + f, where f is the non-interest current account.

  • The root. It sits at about 1 + r_b. G&L's own experiment shows the same thing: the surplus country's current account rises "by ever larger – and accelerating – amounts" (p. 467).
  • The decision (maintainer, 2026-10-01, open question 26): calibrate f to zero, so the baseline is stationary, and document the knife-edge. A scenario that moves the current account restarts the compounding, so under fiat only the baseline can be stationary.
  • No reaction rule inside fiat. That would duplicate OPENFIXR.
  • How it was calibrated (2026-10-01). On the debt/VA slope, by the equity share λ, with the debt endowments then restoring the debt level (they do not move the slope: measured). One instrument cannot zero both the slope and the reserve stock, so the reserves settle away from zero: the EU central bank holds about 21 of RoW bills; the South central bank is short 11.5 of North bills (see Loose ends).
  • Reading a scenario. The change in reserves, and the interest they then compound, carries the external effect; public debt moves only through that interest. The impulse test will show how fast it compounds.

In flight — OPENFLEX, an unstable float

1/1 closed · 100%

One cell is being worked on. The obstacle is a property of the closure's financial dynamics, not a bug.

OPENFLEX (G&L §12.7). Both central banks' reserves are fixed, and the exchange rate clears the market for foreign bills held by households.

  • What was built (2026-10-01): an inner solve, a secant search on xr each period that clears the market to 1e-10 in 4–8 passes, and adaptive expectations of the currency gain.
  • Why expectations. With the realised gain of the same period, the loop is positive and Gauss–Seidel explodes.
  • What happens at full adaptation (θ = 1, σ = 1): the economy solves but cycles explosively, roughly every 6–7 periods with growing amplitude. It is an expectations cobweb.
  • The contrast with the book. In G&L's OPENFLEX the currency depreciates "at a decelerating rate until a new equilibrium is restored" and the fall in output is "only temporary". The difference to find is in the expectations rule, and in the depth of our foreign-bill market (about 66 units of EU holdings against 3,000 RoW bills).
  • Partial adaptation, built 2026-10-01. The expected gain is now a state, E_t = E_{t−1} + θ(g_{t−1} − E_{t−1}), with θ the arm column fx_theta (θ = 1 is the first build). The nine-run scan θ ∈ {1, 0.5, 0.25} × σ ∈ {1, 0.5, 0} finished 2026-10-01 (~14:30): no stable band. θ = 1, σ = 1 stalls at t = 23; the other eight reach t = 100 with xr between 1.3e4 and 2.5e4. |xr − 1| grows 13.8–14.4% per period (OLS slope of log|xr − 1| on t, t = 5..100), a monotonic runaway after t ≈ 20 rather than the cycle seen at θ = 1, σ = 1. At σ = 0 the three θ give identical paths (growth 0.138, xr 22.5 at t = 50): the expected gain enters only through the Tobin returns, so with σ = 0 the divergence is NOT the expectations or portfolio channel; it is the float's own root (the trade/financial response of the bill market to xr). Lower θ and higher σ make it slightly worse (σ = 1: 0.141 at θ = 0.5, 0.144 at θ = 0.25). output/calibration/openflex_theta_sigma_scan_20261001.csv.

Deliberately deferred — OPENFIXR, excluded with reason

2/2 closed · 100%

OPENFIXR (G&L §12.6.1): the adjusting region's bill rate becomes the price that clears its bill market, so a deficit raises the rate until capital inflows stop the reserve loss. Not built (maintainer, 2026-10-01; open question 37). Two reasons:

  • G&L's own result. The rate "has to go on rising for ever": a higher rate raises the interest paid abroad and so widens the deficit it was raised to close. "The attempt to stabilize the system through interest rate changes generates instability" (p. 472). Building it would reproduce a known result.
  • Our model makes it worse. The bill rate sets the rate of profit, r = r_b + e (8.S4), and so every price of production. A rate that drifts upward for ever would carry every relative price with it, and a scenario's price results would mix the circular-economy shock with the monetary closure's runaway.

Its 8 equations stay specified in qmd/equations/closure_overrides.csv (status excluded), and its three cells stay on the grid in grey. Revisit only as a documented experiment, e.g. if a referee asks how monetary policy would respond to a scenario.
