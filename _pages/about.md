    <div class="bio">
      <p>I develop data-driven multi-sector models to understand how the economy, society and the environment interact. My work has appeared in <em>Ecological Economics</em>, the <em>Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization</em>, the <em>Jahrbücher für Nationalökonomie und Statistik</em> and <em>Physical Review E</em>; I co-edited the two volumes of <em>Multiplicity of Time Scales in Complex Systems</em> (Springer/Birkhäuser) and wrote entries for the <em>Dictionary of Ecological Economics</em>; and I have consulted for the World Bank, the Agence Française de Développement and De La Salle University in Manila.</p>
      <p>My research runs in <a href="/research/">three tracks</a>, each defined by its object — ecological macroeconomics, the uneven structure of global production, and an ecological approach to classical political economy — and each worked with three families of method: stock-flow-consistent and multi-regional input–output models; classical and Keynesian dynamical models; and input–output, network and statistical empirics, including machine learning. At the Net Zero Industrial Policy Lab I develop the <a href="https://nzipl-cvce.pages.dev/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Clean Value Chain Explorer</a>, a platform that maps the inputs, trade and competitiveness of clean technologies worldwide.</p>

  <div class="hero-right">
    <div class="section-label">Research Questions</div>
    <ul class="q-list">
      <li>
        <span class="q-dot green"></span>
        Do climate policies centred in the Global North benefit or harm the Global South? When the carbon-importing core decarbonises, who bears the macroeconomic cost in the carbon-exporting periphery?
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="q-dot blue"></span>
        Can states steer a multi-sector economy towards sustainability while keeping employment and prices stable — through price caps, tax-subsidy schemes and industrial policy in strategic sectors?
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="q-dot orange"></span>
        How do shocks and investments propagate through production networks, and what in the structure of an input–output system governs how fast the multiplier process converges?
      </li>
      <li>
        <span class="q-dot purple"></span>
        Are the limits to accumulation set by nature or by distribution? Is degrowth the classical tendency of the rate of profit to fall asserting itself — and does the labour theory of value yield a conservation law of value alongside the irreversibility of production?
      </li>
    </ul>
