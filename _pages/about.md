    <div class="bio">
      <p>I am an ecological macroeconomist who builds data-driven multi-sector models of growth, technical change, and distribution to understand how the economy, society, and the environment interact. Using a wide range of quantitative methods — multi-regional input–output models, dynamical systems, network and statistical analysis of large data sets, including machine learning — I seek to understand how industrial policy can stabilize prices, business fluctuations, and cycles in the face of compounding climate and international risks. My <a href="/research/#multi-regional-stock-flow-consistent-models">job market paper</a> develops a two-region, input–output stock–flow-consistent model to trace how reduced material demand in the North affects production, employment and emissions in the South.</p>

      <p>My work has appeared in <em>Ecological Economics</em>, the <em>Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization</em>, the <em>Jahrbücher für Nationalökonomie und Statistik</em> and <em>Physical Review E</em>. I have consulted for the World Bank and the Agence Française de Développement.</p>

    <div class="section-label">Research Questions</div>
    <div class="q-groups">
      <section class="q-group">
        <h2><a href="/research/#ecological-macroeconomics"><span class="q-dot green"></span>Ecological macroeconomics</a></h2>
        <ul>
          <li>Can ecological gains in the Global North reproduce, under a green label, the asymmetries of ecologically unequal exchange?</li>
          <li>Can industrial policy steer a multi-sector economy towards decarbonization while keeping prices and employment stable?</li>
          <li>How fast can the low-carbon transition proceed, given the different speeds at which prices, quantities and technologies adjust?</li>
          <li>Can public buffer stocks of critical natural resources stabilize prices where price caps and tax-subsidy schemes fail?</li>
        </ul>
      </section>
      <section class="q-group">
        <h2><a href="/research/#uneven-development-and-the-global-structure-of-production"><span class="q-dot orange"></span>Uneven development and global production</a></h2>
        <ul>
          <li>Is world production organized as a core and a periphery, and how do labour, value and natural resources flow between them?</li>
          <li>Do national economies fall into a few prototypes of industrial structure, and do they move between them as they develop?</li>
          <li>Do open borders hurt or benefit workers in the core?</li>
        </ul>
      </section>
      <section class="q-group">
        <h2><a href="/research/#an-ecological-approach-to-classical-political-economy"><span class="q-dot purple"></span>Ecological classical political economy</a></h2>
        <ul>
          <li>Are the limits to economic growth set by nature or by inequality?</li>
          <li>Is value conserved in exchange but created only in production? Which theory of value, utility or labor, offers a coherent analogy to the first and second laws of thermodynamics?</li>
        </ul>
      </section>
